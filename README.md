## Abstract
This paper addresses the challenge of jointly optimizing language model architectures and numeric precision strategies while accounting for training costs, inference latency, and concurrency requirements. Although recent scaling laws (such as the widely cited Chinchilla approach) have guided language model creation by relating parameter counts to data usage, they often overlook the practical cost of large-scale inference. We propose a two-phase method that first identifies Pareto-optimal architectures by weighing cost, concurrency factors, and memory overhead, then applies layer-specific mixed-precision fine-tuning to maintain accuracy while improving performance under dynamic conditions. To validate the effectiveness of our approach, we conduct experiments measuring training cost, per-token inference latency, and throughput across diverse concurrency loads. The results show that searching over parameter count, network shape (width, depth, and attention head configuration), and data usage yields designs that better balance total compute with real-world deployment constraints. Moreover, mixed-precision fine-tuning guided by gradient variance preserves model quality while reducing inference overhead. Our method demonstrates that a holistic optimization strategy is vital for creating language models that meet both training and serving demands in practical environments.
